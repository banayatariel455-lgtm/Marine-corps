About the program

A field discipline, taught like one.

Most media-literacy courses stop at "check your sources." Signal Watch goes further: participants work directly with anonymized case files — coordinated inauthentic campaigns, manipulated media, and astroturfed narratives — under the same time pressure analysts face in the field.

The program was built by former newsroom verification leads and open-source investigators, and is used by newsroom standards desks, election-integrity teams, and corporate trust & safety groups who need staff who can move from "something feels off" to a documented, defensible finding.

Curriculum

Five training modules

Delivered live over six weeks, or as a compressed two-day intensive for teams.

01

Source verification fundamentals

Chain-of-custody thinking for digital claims: primary vs. secondary sourcing, reverse image search, metadata basics, and building a documented trail.

Format: Live + labsDuration: 1 week
02

Narrative & network mapping

How coordinated campaigns spread — identifying amplification patterns, inauthentic clusters, and the difference between organic virality and manufactured reach.

Format: Case labDuration: 1 week
03

Media forensics

Detecting manipulated images, audio, and video — from crude edits to AI-generated synthetic media — using accessible, non-technical checks.

Format: Live + labsDuration: 1 week
04

Writing the finding

Turning an investigation into a clear, defensible report — for a newsroom standards desk, a trust & safety escalation, or a public correction.

Format: WorkshopDuration: 1 week
05

Capstone: live case

Teams work a current, real-world case end to end and present findings to programme mentors for review.

Format: Team projectDuration: 2 weeks
